# Kids Breathing Coach — Animal Breathing Techniques
## Evidence, Mechanics, and Prototype Cadence Review

**Document ID:** KBC-RES-001  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Research complete; prototype prescriptions approved with validation requirements  
**Date:** 2026-09-19

## 1. Executive Finding

Animal breathing is a strong interaction model, but the evidence generally supports the **underlying mechanic**, not the animal-branded technique itself. Snake, bee, cow, duck, bunny, bear, tiger, flower, and candle exercises are mostly teaching metaphors drawn from yoga, occupational therapy, play therapy, mindfulness, classrooms, and parenting resources. Few have been compared directly in pediatric trials.

The app may use creative animal animation when it:

1. preserves a conservative breathing mechanic;
2. avoids holds, maximal inhalation, repeated rapid sniffing, and forceful blowing;
3. presents timing as a flexible animation target rather than a performance requirement;
4. labels benefits as expected experience or product hypothesis, not proven outcome;
5. is tested separately by age band.

## 2. Evidence Hierarchy

| Tier | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| A | Direct pediatric evidence for a closely matching protocol | Limited; pinwheel/controlled exhalation in procedural settings |
| B | Pediatric evidence for the underlying mechanism or broader intervention | Slow breathing, diaphragmatic breathing, HRV biofeedback, mindfulness programs |
| C | Adult/mechanistic evidence plus established pediatric clinical use | Humming/voiced exhale, longer comfortable exhale |
| D | Common educational or traditional practice without direct validation | Most animal names and stories |

No animal technique currently qualifies as a uniquely proven pediatric prescription.

## 3. Cadence Framework

Children naturally breathe faster than adults. New York State pediatric reference material lists resting ranges of roughly **22–34 breaths/minute for preschoolers** and **18–30 for school-age children**. This does not establish a therapeutic pace, but it cautions against automatically applying an adult six-breaths-per-minute prescription to ages 3–6.

### Prototype pacing windows

| Track | Inhale target | Exhale target | Approximate guided rate | Initial dose |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Little Explorers 3–6 | 2–3 sec | 3–4 sec | about 9–12/min | 3–5 cycles |
| Big Explorers 7–12 | 3–4 sec | 4–6 sec | about 6–9/min | 4–8 cycles |

These are conservative **prototype windows**, not medical prescriptions or strict targets. The animation accepts early or late breathing; the child can breathe normally at any time. The initial production default is 3 seconds in and 4 seconds out for Big Explorers, with a less exact visual cue for Little Explorers.

### Engine rules

- No holds.
- Exhale is never more than two seconds longer than inhale in the initial prototype.
- No cycle is repeated automatically beyond the activity's approved dose.
- No child is instructed to empty the lungs.
- Make Easier shortens the exhale and removes precise pacing.
- If voiced or resisted exhale causes strain, switch immediately to normal breathing.

## 4. Technique Review

### 4.1 Bumblebee Breath

**Traditional analogue:** Bhramari/humming breath.  
**Mechanic:** comfortable nasal or oral inhale followed by a soft humming exhale.  
**Evidence grade:** C for acute child product use; limited adjacent evidence for Bhramari and humming physiology.  
**Animation:** bee lands on a flower, takes an easy breath, then hums gently while flying home.

Prototype:

- Little: easy inhale 2–3 sec; hum 3–4 sec; 3 rounds.
- Big: inhale 3 sec; hum 4–5 sec; 4–6 rounds.
- No ear closing, forceful vibration, breath retention, or maximal volume.
- Child may hum with mouth closed or use a quiet voiced sound if nasal breathing is uncomfortable.

**Decision:** Include as a core settling option after supervised comprehension testing.

### 4.2 Snake Breath

**Mechanic:** comfortable inhale followed by a soft `ssss` exhale.  
**Evidence grade:** C for mechanism, D for animal branding.  
**Animation:** snake slowly travels along a curved path as the exhale continues.

Prototype:

- Little: inhale about 2 sec; soft hiss up to 3–4 sec; 3–4 rounds.
- Big: inhale 3 sec; hiss 4–5 sec; 4–6 rounds.
- Sound must remain gentle; the goal is continuity, not longest hiss.

**Decision:** Include. It translates controlled exhalation clearly and visually.

### 4.3 Smell the Flower / Cool the Candle

**Mechanic:** gentle inhale associated with smelling; relaxed exhale associated with moving air.  
**Evidence grade:** B/C for underlying controlled breathing; D for the metaphor. Pinwheel breathing has been used in pediatric play-therapy/procedural research.  
**Animation:** smell a flower, then make a candle flame dance without extinguishing it.

Prototype:

- Little: 2 sec smell; 3 sec gentle candle flicker; 3–4 rounds.
- Big: 3 sec smell; 4–5 sec flicker; 4–6 rounds.
- Use **cool the candle** or **make it dance**, not **blow it out**, to discourage forceful exhalation.
- No required nasal breathing if congested or uncomfortable.

**Decision:** Include as the clearest onboarding technique.

### 4.4 Bear Breath

**Common versions:** slow belly breathing or inhale/hold/exhale/hold in a bear-hibernation story.  
**Evidence grade:** B for gentle slow/diaphragmatic mechanics; D for bear pattern.  
**Animation:** bear's belly rises gently, then settles under a blanket.

Prototype:

- Remove all holds.
- Little: nonnumeric gentle belly rise and fall; 3–4 cycles.
- Big: 3 sec comfortable inhale; 4 sec relaxed exhale; 4–6 cycles.
- Do not require abdominal movement or tell the child their chest movement is wrong.

**Decision:** Include as bedtime/rest option, not a separate claim of effectiveness.

### 4.5 Cow Breath

**Common analogue:** gentle inhale followed by a voiced `moo` exhale; sometimes confused with yoga postures or alternate-nostril practice.  
**Evidence grade:** C for voiced exhale; D for cow branding.  
**Animation:** cow smells grass, then makes a quiet, long moo.

Prototype:

- Little: easy inhale 2 sec; quiet moo 3 sec; 3 rounds.
- Big: inhale 3 sec; voiced exhale 4–5 sec; 4 rounds.
- Avoid loud vocal competition and throat strain.

**Decision:** Include as an optional humorous voiced-exhale variation, not a core distinct prescription.

### 4.6 Duck Breath

**Common analogue:** no standardized evidence-based protocol; often a playful quack or wing movement.  
**Evidence grade:** D.  
**Animation:** duck glides during the inhale and makes one gentle quack on the exhale.

Prototype:

- Use one soft `quaaack` across a comfortable exhale.
- Same cadence window as Cow Breath.
- Avoid repeated short quacks, which can fragment and speed breathing.

**Decision:** Creative variation only. It inherits the voiced-exhale mechanic; do not market Duck Breath as independently evidence-based.

### 4.7 Bunny Breath

**Common versions:** three short nasal sniffs followed by a longer exhale; sometimes marketed as energizing.  
**Evidence grade:** D; repeated rapid inhalations present avoidable overbreathing risk.  
**Animation:** bunny smells one flower, pauses naturally without a prescribed hold, then lets one easy breath out.

Prototype:

- Do not use three repeated sniffs as a looping calming protocol.
- Permitted adaptation: one small sniff-like inhale followed by one normal, relaxed exhale, then return to ordinary breathing.
- Maximum one demonstration plus two child attempts.

**Decision:** Include only as a brief playful reset or smelling activity; not a paced session and not an energy claim.

### 4.8 Tiger/Lion Breath

**Common analogue:** forceful open-mouth exhale, roar, tongue extension, or yoga lion's breath.  
**Evidence grade:** D for child regulation; forceful execution conflicts with MVP constraints.  
**Animation:** tiger stretches and makes a quiet, easy `haa` rather than a maximal roar.

Prototype:

- Prefer as a movement/expressive alternative, not core breathing cadence.
- One comfortable inhale and one nonforceful `haa` exhale.
- No repeated roaring, maximal mouth opening, or instruction to expel all air.

**Decision:** Place under **Move or Express**, not the primary calming library.

## 5. Recommended Animal Library

### Core launch set

1. Flower and Candle — first-use/onboarding
2. Snake — controlled exhale
3. Bumblebee — humming exhale
4. Bear — quiet/bedtime belly animation
5. Bunny — single brief reset, not repeated sniffing
6. Tiger — movement/expression alternative

### Cosmetic variations using an existing mechanic

- Cow inherits Bumblebee's voiced-exhale structure.
- Duck inherits a gentle voiced-exhale structure.
- Additional animals may ship only by mapping to an approved mechanic and dose.

This avoids pretending that every animal requires a different physiological protocol.

## 6. Product Taxonomy

The user chooses an outcome or animal adventure; the engine chooses one of four approved mechanics:

| Mechanic ID | Structure | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| EASY_LONGER_OUT | easy inhale, slightly longer relaxed exhale | Flower/Candle, Bear |
| VOICED_OUT | easy inhale, soft continuous voiced exhale | Bumblebee, Cow, Duck |
| CONTROLLED_OUT | easy inhale, soft continuous unvoiced exhale | Snake |
| SINGLE_RESET | one easy inhale/exhale followed by normal breathing | Bunny, Tiny Reset |

Tiger is an expressive/movement tool rather than a distinct paced-breath mechanic.

## 7. Validation Questions

- Can children follow the animal without overinhaling or straining?
- Does sound production lengthen exhale comfortably or create competition?
- Do younger children interpret candle animation as forceful blowing?
- Does Bunny increase pace or excitement?
- Does humming create sensory discomfort?
- Which animals feel too young for ages 10–12?
- Can children stop without believing the animal is disappointed?
- Does each animal remain understandable with no audio or reduced motion?

## 8. Promotion Rules

An animal moves from prototype to approved content only when:

- at least 80% in each intended age band understand the action;
- fewer than 10% visibly strain, overinhale, repeatedly sniff, or force the exhale;
- no moderate/severe adverse experience occurs;
- Stop and normal-breath recovery work immediately;
- the child does not interpret the activity as a contest;
- the script passes clinical and child-development review.

## 9. Source Basis

- New York State pediatric reference ranges establish that normal respiratory rates differ materially by age: [Pediatric assessment tools](https://www.health.ny.gov/professionals/ems/pdf/assmttools.pdf).
- A pediatric diaphragmatic-breathing review discusses pinwheel breathing as a play-based method and illustrates the broader evidence base: [Tsakona et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/).
- Animal techniques are widely used by early-childhood support programs, but such use is pedagogical evidence rather than proof of a unique physiological effect: [Help Me Grow Utah animal breathing](https://helpmegrowutah.org/).

The evidence supports conservative mechanics and testing. It does not establish a clinical prescription for each named animal.
