# Kids Breathing Coach — Animal Cast and Visual Direction

**Document ID:** KBC-ADR-001  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Accepted for prototype  
**Date:** 2026-09-19

## Decision

Adopt an **ensemble animal cast** in a soft storybook world. Each animal owns one recognizable breathing mechanic or non-breathing alternative. A small firefly named **Pip** serves as the home guide and navigation helper but does not perform every exercise.

## Cast

| Character | Mechanic/role | Personality | Visual motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pip the Firefly | Home guide and transitions | Warm, curious, never needy | Slow glow; points to choices |
| Suki the Snake | Controlled `ssss` exhale | Patient, smooth | Travels along a curved path |
| Bibi the Bumblebee | Soft humming exhale | Cheerful, steady | Rises on inhale, glides while humming |
| Bruno the Bear | Quiet rise/fall and bedtime | Cozy, reassuring | Belly/blanket rises gently |
| Clover the Bunny | Single brief reset | Alert but gentle | Smells one flower; settles |
| Tavi the Tiger | Movement/expression alternative | Playful, bold | Stretch, shake, one soft `haa` |
| Daisy and Candle | First-use teaching scene | Familiar and simple | Petals open; flame dances |

Cow and duck remain optional later cosmetic variations using the voiced-exhale mechanic.

## Art Direction

- Rounded 2D vector/storybook characters with subtle texture
- Expressive silhouettes readable at small phone sizes
- No hyperreal anatomy, glossy 3D toy rendering, or overstimulating motion
- Animals are appealing through age 12: cute but not infant-coded
- Little Explorer mode uses larger faces and more environmental response
- Big Explorer mode uses tighter compositions, subdued expressions, and less narration
- Eyes remain open during active guidance to avoid implying eyes must close

## World

The cast lives around **Quiet Creek**, a small natural environment with meadow, water, tree hollow, night sky, and simple paths. Backgrounds reuse modular layers so the experience feels coherent without requiring unique animation for every screen.

## Emotional Rules

- Characters never become sad when the child stops.
- Characters never say they missed the child or need help from the child.
- No crying, panic, danger, rescue stakes, or failure animation.
- The character practices alongside the child rather than watching or grading.
- Completion acknowledges participation, not emotional change.

## Production Decision

Prototype with simple vector shapes and color-blocked animals. Commission or generate finished character sheets only after the clickable prototype confirms comprehension, age fit, and motion tolerance.

## Rejected Alternatives

- One mascot for every technique: weaker mechanic recognition
- A different art world per exercise: expensive and visually fragmented
- Photoreal animals: less expressive and harder to animate clearly
- Strong game progression: risks pressure and distraction
